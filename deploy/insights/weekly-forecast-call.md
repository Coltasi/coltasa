# Running a weekly forecast call in 30 minutes

URL: https://www.coltasa.com/insights/weekly-forecast-call  
Author: Colin Constantine  
Published: 2026-10-05  
Category: Forecasting

> A simple agenda for a weekly forecast meeting that keeps the number honest without taking over the team's week.

Photo: [Alena Darmel](https://www.pexels.com/photo/group-of-businessmen-and-woman-looking-on-a-laptop-8134098) on Pexels
Weekly forecast calls have a habit of growing. They start at 30 minutes and become a two-hour walk through every deal in the CRM. Sellers dread them, leaders leave without a clear number, and the real problems in the pipeline go unnoticed. It shows in the results: in Gartner's State of Sales Operations survey, [only 45 percent of sales leaders and sellers](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2020-02-12-gartner-says-less-than-50--of-sales-leaders-and-selle) had high confidence in their forecast accuracy.

A forecast call works best when it is short, structured and focused on the deals that move the number.

## Prepare the day before

Ask each seller to update their deals by the end of the previous day. Every deal expected to close in the period needs a category ([commit, best case or pipeline](https://www.coltasa.com/insights/forecast-evidence)), a close date and a one-line next step. The leader reviews the changes in advance, so the call is spent on discussion rather than data entry.

## A 30-minute agenda

- 5 minutes on the number: total commit, best case and gap to target, compared with last week.

- 15 minutes on deals: only those that changed category, moved date or are large enough to change the outcome.

- 5 minutes on risks: each seller names one deal at risk and what would help, such as an executive call or a pricing decision.

- 5 minutes on next quarter: a quick look at [coverage](https://www.coltasa.com/insights/pipeline-coverage) for the following period.

## Focus on what changed

The most useful question in a forecast call is "what changed since last week?" A deal that moves from best case to commit should come with new evidence that it has met your [stage exit criteria](https://www.coltasa.com/insights/stage-exit-criteria), such as an agreed contract date or confirmed budget. A deal that slips should come with a reason. Plan for slippage: [Ebsta’s 2024 benchmarks](https://ebsta.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/H1-Update-2024-B2B-Sales-Benchmarks.pdf) found that a third or more of deals slipped past their close date each quarter in the first half of that year. Deals that have not changed do not need discussion.

## Ask the same questions every time

Consistency makes the call faster and fairer. For each deal discussed:

- What did the buyer do this week?

- What is the next step, and is it in the calendar?

- Who signs, and have we spoken to them?

- What could stop this closing on time?

## Keep coaching separate

When a deal needs real work, such as rethinking the approach or preparing for a difficult negotiation, move it to a separate deal review. Coaching in the forecast call slows everything down and puts the seller on the spot in front of the team.

## Record the numbers

Write down commit and best case each week, by seller and in total. Over a few quarters this shows how accurate each person's calls are and whether the team leans optimistic or cautious. That history makes future forecasts much more reliable.

## Make honesty easy

The call only works if people raise bad news early. Thank sellers who move a deal down when the evidence changes. If moving a deal out of commit leads to a difficult conversation, people will hold off until it is too late to help.

Run this way, the forecast call becomes a useful 30 minutes rather than the meeting everyone tries to avoid.
